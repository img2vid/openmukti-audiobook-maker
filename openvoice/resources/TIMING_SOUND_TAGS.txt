OPENMUKTI AUDIO TIMELINE / PAUSE / CHARACTER / IPA / SOUND-EFFECT TAG MANUAL
Version 1.0.27 — also available in Help > Complete User Manual
=====================================================================

WHAT THE TAGS DO
----------------
Square brackets direct speech timing, voices, sound effects and overlapping
character dialogue. Tags do not themselves get spoken. Imported sounds live in
the current project and are included when exporting a complete .oamproj file.
The timed audio renderer uses FFmpeg, which must be available. Ordinary text
without timing tags continues through the familiar sequential voice renderer.

Voice models do not automatically change their pronunciation to make a line
last a certain duration. Requested speaking time is accomplished by time-
stretching synthesized audio using FFmpeg's atempo filter. Extreme adjustments
may sound artificial. Supported speed ratio is 0.1× to 10×. Timing refers to
the final mixed audio clock, not synchronized mouth movements in a video.

1. CHARACTER / NARRATOR SELECTION
----------------------------------
[Mary]Hello!
[John]How are you?
[Narrator]The conversation continued.
[Speaker: Mary]Hello!
[Character: Mary]Hello!
[spoken by Mary]Hello!
[narrated by Mary]Hello!
[Mary speaks]Hello!
[Mary says]Hello!
[Mary is speaking]Hello!
[Mary talking]Hello!
[Mary, duration: 3s]Hello!
[spoken by Mary, speak for 3 seconds]Hello!
[Let Mary speak, pause for 2 seconds]Hello!

Speaker names must exist in Character Voice Studio; aliases work with the
normal speaker-tag syntax. A speaker remains selected until a new speaker tag.
Use the standard [Mary] format for maximum compatibility. The text following
any recognized time/voice tag belongs to the selected current speaker.

2. DURATION NOTATION (MILLISECONDS THROUGH HOURS)
--------------------------------------------------
All the following represent supported time expressions. Numerals may be
integers or decimals. An ASCII space between a number and the unit is optional:

520ms                 520 milliseconds          520 millisecond
520 msec              520 msecs                 520 ms
0.52s                 0.52 seconds              0.52 sec
0.52 secs             half a second             0.5 second
2s                    2 seconds                 two seconds
3.19s                 3.19 seconds              3190 milliseconds
1m                    1 min                     1 minute
1mins                 1 minutes                 1.5 minutes
3m 19s                3 minutes 19 seconds      3min19sec
2h                    2 hr                      2 hours
1h 20m                1 hour 20 minutes         1h20m30s
0.5hr                 half an hour              1.25 hours
00:02                 00:02.500                 01:02:03
01:02:03.190          .25s                      0.25 sec

Clock notation is MM:SS[.fraction] or HH:MM:SS[.fraction]. Clock seconds
and minutes must be under 60. For units, suffixes are case-insensitive.
A maximum individual time of 24 hours applies. Negative time and unknown
unit names are rejected. Time ranges use a hyphen, en dash, em dash,
"to" or "..". For an SFX range, end must exceed start.

3. PAUSES BEFORE A LINE
-----------------------
[Mary][pause: 2s]Hi!
[Mary][p:0.9s]Hi!
[Mary][pause 2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][p=2s]Hi!
[Mary][pause for 2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][wait for 2000ms]Hi!
[Mary][wait: 2s]Hi!
[Mary][silence: 2s]Hi!
[Mary][delay: 2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][remain silent for 2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][be silent for 2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][2 seconds]Hi!
[Mary][2s]Hi!
[Mary][00:02]Hi!
[Mary][1m 20s]Hi!
[Mary][120000ms]Hi!
[Mary, pause: 2s]Hi!
[Mary, wait for two seconds]Hi!
[pause: 2s][Mary]Hi!

When a bare time is in its OWN square bracket, it means a pause. A pause
occurs before the next speech section and is silent unless an SFX is scheduled
within it. Multiple pause tags accumulate. The between-passage gap from the
existing app settings is additional and remains in effect.

4. TIMED SPEAKING / A TARGET SPEECH LENGTH
------------------------------------------
[Mary, 3s]Hi!
[Mary, duration: 3s]Hi!
[Mary, speech duration: 3 seconds]Hi!
[Mary, speaking duration 3 seconds]Hi!
[Mary, speak for 3 seconds]Hi!
[Mary, say this in 3s]Hi!
[Mary, length=3s]Hi!
[Mary, take 3 seconds]Hi!
[Mary, fit to 3s]Hi!
[Mary][duration: 3s]Hi!
[Mary][speak for 3s]Hi!
[Mary][length: 3s]Hi!

A bare duration INSIDE THE SAME bracket after the character name means
speech time rather than pause time, when it is the ONLY bare time there.
This distinction is necessary to support both [Mary, 3s] and [Mary][3s].
Speech duration stretches or compresses the line; it is not a pause.

5. COMBINED PAUSE AND SPEAKING DURATION
--------------------------------------
[Mary, 520ms, 3.19s]Please come to my party.
[Mary, pause: 520ms, duration: 3.19s]Please come to my party.
[Mary][pause: 520ms][speak for 3.19 seconds]Please come to my party.
[spoken by Mary, wait for 520 milliseconds, speech duration 3.19 seconds]Please come.
[Mary, 00:00.520, 00:03.190]Please come.

When two bare durations occur within one bracket, the first denotes the
pre-speech pause; the next denotes the speech target. For clarity, explicit
"pause:" and "duration:" are preferable in longer complex scripts.

6. IMPORTING SOUND EFFECTS INTO THE PROJECT
-------------------------------------------
Open the main window. Under VOICE SETTINGS choose:
  Import / manage project sound effects
Choose one or more audio files (.wav, .mp3, .m4a, .flac, .ogg, .opus,
.aac, .aiff or .wma). The imported filename is the reference used by tags.
Filenames must be unique in the project, ignoring letter case. To remove an
asset, select it and click Remove selected. Removing an import does not delete
the original audio file. Keep imported files available until you export the
complete project; the .oamproj archive embeds the actual sound content.
An .oamproj re-import restores assets to the project workspace. Reopened
projects do not require the original filesystem path.

Use ordinary exact filenames, for instance "door.mp3" and "rain.wav".
A tag may NOT load an arbitrary disk file unless it has been imported into
the project. If a file is not imported, the app reports an error. This limits
unintended filesystem access and makes projects portable.

7. SOUND EFFECTS IN A PAUSE
---------------------------
[Mary, 520ms, sfx:door.mp3(200ms-510ms), 3.19s]Hello!
[Mary, pause: 520ms, sound effect:door.mp3(200ms-510ms), duration:3.19s]Hi!
[Mary, wait for 520ms, audio:door.mp3(200ms to 510ms), 3.19s]Hello!
[Mary, 520ms, play:door.mp3(0.2s-0.51s), 3.19s]Hello!

An effect specified AFTER a pause field but BEFORE the speech-duration field
is anchored to the beginning of the pause. In the first example the effect
starts 200 ms into the pause and occupies 310 ms; Mary starts speech 520 ms
after the preceding passage. Effects are mixed OVER the silence, not as
replacement content. Effects whose range exceeds the pause are rejected.

8. SOUND EFFECTS WHILE SOMEONE SPEAKS
-------------------------------------
[Mary, duration: 3.19s, sfx:birds.wav(2s-3s)]Hello!
[Mary, 3.19s, sound effect:birds.wav(2s to 3s)]Hello!
[Mary, 3.19s, play:birds.wav(2000ms-3000ms)]Hello!
[Mary, 3.19s, audio:birds.wav(00:02..00:03)]Hello!
[Mary, 3.19s, sfx:birds.wav(0s-1s), sfx:bell.mp3(2s-3s)]Hello!

An effect specified AFTER the speech-duration field is anchored to the
start of the speech, so the audio plays during the character's speech.
The interval in parentheses is on the narration timeline (start–end), not
a sample range taken from the source file. The effect is played from the
beginning for the requested interval. Without a specified range, the sound
is limited to the remaining part of the pause or speech. Different effects
can be mixed at the same time.

9. COMPLETE COMBINED EXAMPLE
----------------------------
[Alice, 520ms, sfx:abc.mp3(200ms-510ms), 3.19s, sfx:def.mp3(2s-3s)]Please will you go to my party?

- Alice is the speaking character.
- Silent pre-speech period is 0.520 second.
- abc.mp3 plays from 0.200 to 0.510 second in that pause.
- Alice starts at 0.520 second and finishes at 3.710 seconds.
- Her synthesized utterance is fitted to 3.190 seconds.
- def.mp3 plays from 2.000 to 3.000 seconds after Alice begins speaking.
- abc.mp3 and def.mp3 must both have been imported into the project.

10. BACKGROUND AMBIENCE / STREET NOISES / LOOPING
-------------------------------------------------
[background:street.mp3]
[Narrator]The city was loud. [Mary]Let's cross the street.

[ambient:rain.wav][Mary]It has started raining again.
[ambience:forest.ogg][John]Did you hear the birds?
[loop:traffic.mp3][Mary]Watch out!
[background sound:street.wav][Narrator]Morning rush hour.

Background audio is looped to the length of the rendered dialogue, at a lower
mixing gain (22%). It is placed on an independent track. One-shot sound effects
remain independent from background ambience. If you need a precise ambience
fade envelope, or individual stereo automation, prepare a sound file with that
envelope before importing it; this tag set does not synthesize an envelope.

11. OVERLAPPING DIALOGUE (CHARACTERS SPEAK AT ONCE)
---------------------------------------------------
[Alice]The answer is— [John, overlap: 500ms]Wait, listen to me!
[Mary]I need to say something. [John, speak over: 1s]So do I!
[Mary, duration:4s]Our plan is simple. [John, overlap 2s]Really?
[Mary]Hello. [John, simultaneous]Hi!

"overlap" moves the NEW line earlier by the requested time relative to the
end of the preceding line, keeping the previous line audible. Overlap is
bounded at the beginning of the track (time 0). The shorter overlapping
interval depends on how long the generated clips last.

12. INTERRUPTIONS / CUTTING OFF A SPEAKER
----------------------------------------
[Alice, duration:4s]I was about to tell you what—
[John, interrupt after 1.3s]Stop! I know already.

[Alice]This is my explanation.
[John, cut off after 350ms]No, let me speak.

[Alice]Please listen!
[John, stop previous at 0.7s]Never mind.

An interruption starts a NEW speaker at the given offset FROM THE PREVIOUS
speaker's START and trims the previous speaker's WAV at that same point. The
previous speaker's source text is still synthesized in full before trimming.
You cannot know in advance exactly which word will be interrupted because
different model voices and pronunciations yield different speaking speeds.
For word-accurate interruptions, divide the prior line into smaller segments
and place interruption markers at the intended segment boundary.

13. IPA + CHARACTER + PAUSE / SOUND EFFECT
-----------------------------------------
[Alice][ipa: həˈloʊ|hello]!
[Alice, duration:2s]<ipa:həˈloʊ|hello> there!
[spoken by Alice, pause for 250ms][ipa: bɔ̃ʒuʁ|bonjour]!
[Alice, 300ms, sfx:click.wav(0ms-100ms), duration:1s]<ipa:həˈloʊ|hello>.

The bracket style [ipa: phonemes|spoken fallback] is converted to the
application's existing <ipa:phonemes|spoken fallback> format. The spoken
fallback is necessary for engines which do not synthesize direct IPA. The
older <ipa:...> notation, including language-aware dictionaries, still works.
IPA and speaker tags can appear in any order as long as they form supported
fields. Avoid nested square brackets. Angle-bracket IPA can be mixed with
timeline tags and is useful when pronunciation should not be visible.

14. FORMAL GRAMMAR AND COMBINATIONS
-----------------------------------
  script        := (text | '[' fields ']')*
  fields        := field (',' field | ';' field)*
  field         := character | pause | speech_duration | bare_duration
                 | sfx | background | overlap | interruption | ipa
  character     := known-name | 'Speaker: ' known-name
                 | 'Character: ' known-name | 'spoken by ' known-name
  pause         := ('pause'|'p'|'wait'|'delay'|'silence'|... ) ':'? time
  speech_duration := ('duration'|'speech duration'|'speak for'|...) ':'? time
  bare_duration := time
  sfx           := ('sfx:'|'sound effect:'|'audio:'|'play:') filename
                   [ '(' time '-' time ')' ]
  background    := ('background:'|'ambient:'|'ambience:'|'loop:') filename
  overlap       := ('overlap'|'talk over'|'speak over') ':'? time
  interruption  := ('interrupt'|'cut off'|'stop previous') ['after'|'at'] time
  ipa           := 'ipa:' phonemes [ '|' spoken_fallback ]
  time          := (number unit)+ | HH:MM:SS[.fraction] | MM:SS[.fraction]

For example these are valid:
[Alice, pause:2s, duration:3s]Hi.
[Alice; pause for 2 seconds; speech duration 3 seconds]Hi.
[spoken by Alice, 2s, 3s, sfx:door.mp3(0.5s to 1.0s)]Hi.
[Mary][pause:1m][duration:2.5s]Good morning.
[ambient:traffic.wav][Alice, 520ms, sfx:click.wav(0ms-100ms), 3s]Hello.
[Bob, overlap:2s, duration:4s]I'm speaking at the same time!
[Bob, interrupt after 700ms, duration:1.5s]Wait!

A bracketed tag containing no recognized directive is preserved as text.
Unrecognized extra fields inside an otherwise recognized directive produce
a clear error instead of silently shifting the timeline.
This is a defined subset of English command phrasing, not an unrestricted
natural-language AI instruction interpreter. Avoid ambiguous free-form prose
inside tags. There are many supported spellings and combined forms, but not
every conceivable sentence in English can be interpreted deterministically.

15. NOTES AND LIMITATIONS
-------------------------
- FFmpeg is required for timed/overlapping/rendered sound scenes. It may
  be bundled by the corresponding build or installed on the system.
- A maximum of 256 simultaneous timeline clips per render is enforced.
  Split exceptionally long scenes into chapters/sections.
- The max duration per tag/scene is 24 hours. Large audio may require RAM
  and disk space. The app does not download sounds or copyrighted recordings.
- Starting playback with Speak uses the same rendered WAV as Export Audio.
- Video creation uses that mixed WAV as its soundtrack. Captions display
  the dialogue text and do not include timing markup.
- Audio may sound unnatural at extreme time-stretch speeds.
- Character/IPA model quality depends on the selected installed voice.
- Speech without explicit timing continues using existing sentence pauses.
- Imported SFX are included in .oamproj but original external locations are
  still used until the project is exported and reopened.
- Entire-project export bundles sound files, not non-Piper neural model weights.
- Manual Piper/Neural voice downloads remain separate from SFX importing.
- For major narration projects, listen to the finished mix and verify
  sound levels and timing before publishing it.
